## Channel 1 — Instagram DM (your main channel)

### Read this before you send one DM

**A new Instagram account sending cold DMs gets banned within a day.** This is the single way this plan fails.

Rules that are not optional:

- **Use an aged account.** 6+ months old, real profile photo, 15+ posts, some real followers. If you don't have one, start warming one today and lean on phone for week 1.
- **Never use automation tools.** Instagram detects them and bans permanently. Every DM is sent by hand.
- **Ramp slowly:** 5–10/day week 1 → 15/day week 2 → 25/day cap. Never exceed 25/day on one account.
- **If Instagram shows "Action Blocked" — stop completely for 48 hours.** Pushing through gets the account killed.

Three aged accounts × 25/day = 75 DMs/day. One account = 25/day.

### The warm-up trick that triples your reply rate

Cold DMs from a non-follower land in **Message Requests** — a folder most people rarely open. Don't send cold.

**Day 1:** Follow them. Like 2–3 recent posts. View their story.
**Day 2:** Send the DM.

Now your name is familiar when the request appears. This takes reply rates from ~5% to **15–20%**. It is the difference between this working and not working.

Do the engagement pass on tomorrow's 25 while you DM today's 25. Costs you 20 extra minutes a day.

### The DM sequence

**DM 1** — after engaging
> Hi [Name] — saw your [specific thing from their page: veneer case, clinic tour, before/after]. Genuinely good work.
>
> I build websites for dental practices. I rebuilt yours as a sample this weekend — it's live now. Want the link? Free, I just want your honest opinion on it.

**DM 2** — 48 hrs, no reply
> Here it is anyway: [link]
>
> Main change is booking — one tap on mobile instead of a contact form. Have a look on your phone 👍

**DM 3** — 4 days later
> All good if the timing's wrong. It's yours either way, I built it for you.

Then stop. Never a fourth.

### Why this beats your email

You reference their actual work. You already built the thing. You give before you ask. And it appears on the app they check thirty times a day, not an inbox with a spam filter.

### Priority order

**Start with the 993 "NO PHONE — DM only" leads that have no website.** They can't be called, so competitors don't call them, and they have no site at all — no rebuild objection. Several have 20,000–235,000 followers. A dentist with 235k followers and no website is the easiest sale in your entire database.

### Gatekeeper script

> "Hi, this is Milin — I'm not selling anything over the phone, I just need thirty seconds. I redesigned your practice's website and put it online so Dr. [Name] could take a look. Is he/she around, or should I send the link to you?"

"Not selling anything" + "already built it" + an easy exit. You're giving, not asking.

### When you reach the dentist

> "Dr. [Name] — quick one. I build websites for dental practices and I rebuilt yours over the weekend as a sample. It's live right now, you can pull it up on your phone. Booking takes one tap instead of a contact form.
>
> Can I text you the link? Look at it, and if you hate it, delete it — costs you nothing."

Then **stop talking.** Let them speak first.

**"How much?"**
> "$597 for the full build, live in seven days. But look at it first — I don't want you paying for something you haven't seen."

**"We already have a website."**
> "I know, I've been on it — that's how I rebuilt yours. The problem isn't that it exists. On mobile a patient scrolls past four screens before finding how to book, and most give up. Let me send the link, thirty seconds."

**"Send me an email."**
> "Will do. What's the best address? And can I call you Thursday afternoon for your thoughts — or is Friday better?"

Always leave with a booked next step.

## Channel 3 — Website contact forms (the spam-filter bypass)

This is the clever one, and almost nobody does it.

Around 5,000 of your leads have a website with a contact form. **When you submit that form, their own website sends the email — from their own domain, to their own inbox.** It lands in the same folder as patient enquiries, because that's exactly what it looks like to their mail server.

**It cannot be spam-filtered.** You have completely sidestepped the problem that killed your last campaign.

**What to write:**

> Name: Milin
> Email: [your address]
> Message: Hi — not a patient enquiry, apologies. I build websites for dental practices and I rebuilt yours as a free sample. It's live here: [link]. Have a look, and if it's not for you just ignore this. Either way it's yours.

Short, honest, opens with an apology for the wrong channel. That honesty is why it gets read.

**30 forms/day, by hand.** Slower than DMs but near-100% delivery — the highest of any channel you have.
