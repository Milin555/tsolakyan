## Verdict first

This one doesn't deserve deletion. Out of the eight I got this week, seven opened with "I hope this finds you well" and a Loom link. This one names my actual Linktree. That's enough to buy sentence two. But it has one strategic error that costs it a reply, and three small tells that mark it as automated.

---

**1. The exact word I stop at: "nothing."**

> "so {{practice}} has **nothing** for Google to show and no page that takes a booking."

Sentence one earns the read. Then "so" pivots and I get a two-clause list of what I lack — Google, bookings. That's the exact shape of the other seven emails. My eyes slide to the link and I decide from there. Everything between "goes to Linktree" and the URL is dead weight I skim past.

**2. What makes it read mass-sent**

- `"Your name, your logo, your colours, real pages."` — the four-beat list is copywriter rhythm, not a person typing. And "real pages" means nothing. Real as opposed to what?
- `"say so and I'll stop."` — stop *what*? That's the moment I learn there's a queue behind this. You've told me I'm in a sequence before I've clicked anything.
- Subject 2, `{{practice}} — I built you a website`. My practice name followed by an em dash is the mail-merge fingerprint. Every automated tool does that. So does your body copy: `{{first_name}} —`. Two merge-dashes in one email.

**3. Is the observation credible? Yes. Is it useful? No.**

Naming my Instagram handle and my Linktree proves you looked. Nothing blasted to 10,000 people can do that. Credibility: full marks.

But here's the problem — **I already know I don't have a website.** I chose Linktree. You're telling me my own decision back to me and calling it a finding. It's accurate and it's not news. The whole first paragraph informs me of something I did on purpose.

The one line in the entire sequence that told me something I *hadn't* thought about is buried in Touch 2: **"can't take a Tuesday-night booking."** That's the moment I picture — the woman who called at 8pm, got voicemail, and booked with someone else. Move it to Touch 1 and cut the Google clause.

**4. Would I click? Yes — with one condition.**

Curiosity about my own logo beats my cynicism. That's a real advantage and you should keep leaning on it.

But two risks you're carrying:

- `milin.dev/d/willow-lane` is a domain I've never seen, from a stranger, and my practice sat through a phishing training in March. Say what's behind it. "It's a webpage, nothing to sign into" costs six words and recovers the clicks from the cautious half.
- If I open it on my phone and it's visibly a template with my logo pasted on, you're dead in three seconds and Touch 2 and 3 are wasted. The demo has to survive that glance. That's not a copy problem but it's the whole bet.

**5. The single sentence that gets a reply**

Not one that's in there. It's this: **"one payment, you own the domain and the files, no monthly fee."**

You've misread why I'm on Linktree. It's not laziness. It's that the last web guy charged me £180/month, held my domain, and when I left I got nothing. Everyone I know has a version of that story. Ownership is the objection. Address it and I reply.

**6. The price is in the wrong touch. This is the big one.**

Your note says a number in Touch 1 "turns it into a quote before they've seen anything." I understand the reasoning and I think it's wrong here — because $495 is *low*.

When someone says "I built you a website" and doesn't name a price, I fill in the blank myself, and I fill it in with $4,000, because that's what the last three quotes were. Then I don't click, because I'm not starting a sales conversation I can't afford between patients.

$495 is your best asset and you're benching it for three days. It's not a quote, it's a disqualifier — it tells me this is a decision I can make alone, without my practice manager, without a meeting. Put it in Touch 1.

**7. Condescending / threatening**

Not threatening — you've kept fear out completely and that's genuinely well done. No "patients are choosing your competitor," no compliance scare. Rare.

Two small things:

- `"There's no website **of your own** behind it"` — "of your own" is faintly parental. Cut three words, problem gone.
- `"if it's wrong for you, say so and I'll stop"` puts me in the position of having to actively reject a person. Small guilt lever. I'd rather you point at unsubscribe so ignoring you is a valid answer.

And Touch 3: `"good luck with the practice"` from a stranger who has emailed me three times reads sarcastic on a phone. Cut it. `"I'll leave you alone"` also reminds me I was being bothered — don't name the thing.

**8. What beats this**

Three things, in order:

1. **A named local reference.** "I did Northgate Dental on Harrogate Road — here's their site." One nearby practice name beats every clever line in this email. I will click that before I click my own demo.
2. **Done-ness.** "The domain yourpracticename.co.uk is available, I've built the site, $495 and it's live Thursday." Not a proposal — a thing that already exists and needs a yes.
3. **Price and proof in the first email.** Anyone who gives me the demo, the number, and one real example in one screen beats a three-touch drip, because I only read email once.

**What changed and why**

- **Google is gone.** Every agency email says Google. The Tuesday-night booking is the same point made as a picture instead of a category.
- **"I built it from a template, which is why it's $495 and not $5,000."** This is the line I'd actually reply to. It's true, no agency on earth would say it, it explains a suspiciously low price before I decide the catch is hidden, and it makes everything else in the email more believable. Your instinct to volunteer Surat is the same instinct — extend it to the method.
- **Price in Touch 1**, framed as ownership, not as a quote. Sequence still works: Touch 2 becomes the Tuesday-night story expanded plus "still live," Touch 3 unchanged.
- **"nothing to sign into"** recovers the phishing-cautious clicks.
- **Unsubscribe is the exit**, so ignoring me is a legitimate answer and I don't have to reject a human being between patients.
- **Cut:** "of your own," "real pages," the four-beat list, "say so and I'll stop."
- **Signature reads as a person**, not a passport stamp. Keep Surat. It's the second most credible thing in the email after my own Instagram handle.

**One flag on Touch 3:** your own note is right and you should enforce it in the pipeline, not in the copy review. If the demo doesn't expire on a real timer, `Taking the demo down Friday` is the single line that would make me mark this as spam — not because it's pushy, but because I'd check on Saturday and find it still up.
